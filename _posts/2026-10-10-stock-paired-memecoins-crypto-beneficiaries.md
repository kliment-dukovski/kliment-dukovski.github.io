During previous crypto cycles, meme coins had no intrinsic value. They were mostly propped up by community, hype, and perhaps humor. The game in 2026 has changed. We’re now seeing stock-paired meme coins that reward holders with tokenized versions of the underlying stock without staking, looping, or any other action.

However, thousands of meme coins launch daily, making it almost impossible to know which, if any, will win the narrative this cycle. Instead of trying to find the needle in the meme coin haystack, you could play this cycle by investing in the companies that power the infrastructure behind this new meta. This includes meme coin launchpads, liquidity providers and automated market makers (AMMs), tokenized stock issuers, and exchanges.

Ironically, decentralized finance and the move away from traditional finance may prop up traditional stocks too if demand for these stock-paired meme coins increases during this cycle.

## Key Takeaways

- A stock-paired meme coin is launched on a specialized platform such as StonkFun, Pons, or Pump.fun. These platforms earn fees during the launch and thereafter. They use a portion of these fees to buy back their native tokens, STONK, PONS, and PUMP, thereby increasing demand.

- Newly launched meme coin tokens can have no tax or a transfer tax of up to 3%. Every time a token with a transfer tax is bought, sold, or transferred from a user’s wallet, the tax is deducted from the transfer amount. If the coin creator chooses to, the tax can be used to buy the paired tokenized stock, passively rewarding eligible holders and potentially driving demand for this type of meme coin.

- Eligible token holders receive tokenized stock based on their holdings, automatically purchased with the token tax. Typically, to receive the reward, you must hold at least $20 of the meme coin. If the value falls below that threshold, meme coin holders receive nothing.

- Raydium is an AMM that provides liquidity for these meme coins. Users on Meteora and Orca can provide concentrated (CLMM) or dynamic (DLMM) liquidity and earn fees on every swap. Raydium, Meteora, and Orca buy back their RAY, MET, and ORCA tokens with their revenue.

- Decentralized exchanges (DEXs) like Jupiter earn fees when a trade uses Jupiter’s route. Jupiter also buys back its JUP token using fee revenue.

- Companies like Ondo and Backpack tokenize stocks and bring them on-chain. However, these two companies don’t buy back tokens, so any benefit to ONDO and BP from this stock-paired meme coin meta would be indirect.

## What is a stock-paired meme coin?

A stock-paired meme coin is a crypto token whose price is quoted in a stock-linked token rather than SOL or USDC. For example, you have a Super Inu meme coin (SI) launched on StonkFun. SI is paired with NVDAx, the tokenized version of Nvidia.

Although SI is paired with NVDAx in both the initial liquidity pool and the reward mechanism, that doesn’t mean you can only buy SI with NVDAx. You can use SOL or USDC to buy SI, or any other meme coin paired with tokenized stocks. DEXs like [Jupiter](<https://klimentdukovski.com/articles/jupiter-jup-best-altcoin-next-crypto-cycle/>) handle everything in the background, including swapping your SOL or USDC into the quoted tokenized stock to buy the meme coin.

Some of these meme coins can impose a transfer tax of up to 3%. This means that whenever someone buys, sells, or sends the token to another wallet, the tax is deducted from the amount. Sometimes this transfer tax appears as a warning on DEXs, but that’s the feature, not the bug.

The tax is then used to buy the quoted token, such as NVDAx, which is distributed to eligible holders. Meme coins launched on StonkFun typically require a minimum holding of $20 worth of the meme coin to earn the quoted token as a reward. Rewards are distributed in proportion to the holdings. But if the value of the holdings drops under $20, the holder stops getting rewards.

In addition to stocks, meme coins can be paired with:

- Tokenized pre-stocks (companies that have yet to go public), such as OpenAI, Anthropic, and Neuralink.

- Tokenized commodities like gold and oil.

- Other crypto coins like SOL, ZEC, and BTC.

- Stablecoins like USDC, EURC, and USDT.

- Leveraged crypto coins like 3x SOL and 3x BTC.

### Tokenized stock issuers

Some of the main beneficiaries in this new stock-paired meme-coin meta are tokenized stock issuers. This creates new revenue streams, including minting and redemption fees, management and administrative fees, and trading fees.

While the hype is currently on meme-coin pairings, tokenized stocks by themselves are a massive market, especially for users in countries that find it hard to open US brokerage accounts and buy US stocks. Beyond unlocking new regions, tokenized stocks can trade 24/7 and serve as collateral in decentralized finance (DeFi) apps.

Several companies already issue tokenized stocks, including Backpack, Backed Assets (xStocks), Ondo Finance, Coinbase, and Robinhood. The NYSE owner, Intercontinental Exchange, [partnered with crypto exchange OKX](<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-05/okx-files-with-sec-to-launch-tokenized-us-stock-trading-platform>) to launch a tokenized stock-trading platform. As demand grows, more companies and exchanges will likely follow suit.

While the core promise is to create tokenized on-chain versions of traditional stocks, issuers differ in how they handle redemptions and what their tokenized stocks represent. For instance, [Backpack’s tokenized stocks](<https://support.backpack.exchange/backpack-securities/tokenized-securities>) are on-chain versions of stocks held in Backpack Securities and are redeemable 1:1.

Traditional shares on Backpack are held through brokerage infrastructure as security entitlements governed by New York law (UCC Article 8) and connected via a two-way door. This allows users to seamlessly convert their tokenized versions to real stocks and vice versa.

For Backpack’s tokenized securities, cash dividends are automatically reinvested into additional tokenized shares.

[Backed Asset’s xStocks](<https://docs.xstocks.fi/docs/how-xstocks-work>) function similarly, including dividend reinvestment and conversion into actual brokerage-held shares, but they differ in legal structure.

Unlike the previous two, [Ondo Finance](<https://ondo.finance/ondo-stocks>) tokenized stocks provide stock-backed economic exposure, but you don’t have the right to receive the underlying shares. Instead, when you redeem, you receive the underlying value in stablecoins.

## Memecoin launchpads

Tokenizing stocks and pairing them with memecoins is the bread and butter of token launchpads like Pump.fun, StonkFun, and Pons. These platforms let anyone create a meme coin, pair it with supported tokenized stocks or other assets, and launch it in a few clicks at minimal cost.

This is one reason thousands of new meme coins launch daily. That’s good for launchpads, which earn fees. For example, on Pump.fun, each trade on the bonding curve [costs 1.25%](<https://pump.fun/docs/fees>), with 0.95% going to the launchpad and the remainder to the token creator. If the meme coin graduates, PumpSwap, Pump.fun’s native DEX, continues collecting protocol fees.

<aside class="article-callout" aria-label="Memecoin launch terms explained" markdown="1">

### Memecoin launch terms explained

**A bonding curve** is an algorithmic pricing mechanism that links a meme coin’s price to its circulating supply via a mathematical formula. The idea is to eliminate the need for a traditional order book or a pre-funded liquidity pool while the token is in its infancy. When someone buys the meme coin during this phase, their SOL is added to the reserve, and meme coins are removed, pushing the token price up. When someone sells, they return the tokens to the reserve and withdraw SOL, pushing the token price down. During this phase, there are no counterparties; everyone buys and sells against the smart contract.

**Graduation** occurs when enough traders buy a meme coin to push its market cap to a specific target, for example, 85 SOL. Once that happens, the bonding curve ends, and the meme coin moves to a DEX with its own liquidity pools. The smart contract uses the accumulated funds (for example, 85 SOL) to fund the initial pool and deposit them into a DEX pool like Raydium. At this stage, everyone can buy the meme coin on a DEX.

</aside>

StonkFun charges a 1% trading fee during the bonding curve stage, takes 0.75% of it, and leaves 0.25% for Raydium, according to [Bitquery’s API documentation](<https://docs.bitquery.io/docs/blockchain/Solana/stonkfun-api/>). After token graduation, StonkFun continues to earn LP fees.

Pons is a popular launchpad on the Robinhood Chain that earns launch fees and a share of trading fees. The [platform earns 0.0005 ETH](<https://ponsfamily.com/analytics>) per launch, 1% of V1 pool trading volume, V2 curve fees, creator fees, and graduated-pool fees as they are collected.

## Liquidity providers and platforms

Automated Market Makers (AMMs) such as Raydium, Orca, and Uniswap, along with Pump’s bonding-curve liquidity, make it easy to launch stock-paired meme coins. Once a token graduates, anyone can open a liquidity pool on these platforms and earn a share of trading fees.

These AMMs earn swap fees when trades pass through their pools. [Raydium splits pool trading fees,](<https://docs.raydium.io/ray/protocol-fees>) allocating 84% to LPs, 12% to RAY buybacks, and 4% to the treasury.

[Uniswap earns swap fees](<https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/get-started/concepts/fees>) and protocol fees. V2 charges a flat 0.30% fee, automatically added to the pool reserve, while V3 uses fee tiers of 0.05%, 0.30%, and 1%. However, custom V4 hooks can redirect fees elsewhere. Pons V2 charges through its own hook and has zero core pool fees, so Uniswap may not receive part of each Pons trade.

Meteora, on the other hand, [retains 10% of trading fees](<https://docs.meteora.ag/protocol/protocol-revenues>) for standard DLMM market-maker liquidity, 20% for DLMM launch pools, and 20% for DAMM V2 pools. The remaining fees go to LPs or other recipients.

Orca benefits from tokenized stocks by collecting fees whenever those tokens trade through its pools, as do the other platforms.

## Trading platforms and DEXs

While you can buy these meme coins directly on their launchpads or on dedicated trading [apps like Fomo](<https://klimentdukovski.com/articles/fomo-app-review/>), you'll likely get a better deal by trading the token on a decentralized exchange. Options include Raydium, Orca, and Jupiter.

Jupiter’s DEX lets users swap any asset for the meme coin, including USDC, SOL, and other meme coins. Users can also set limit orders, dollar-cost average into their position, or set stop-loss and take-profit orders, a feature that other DEXs lack.

Users on Jupiter pay ultra swap fees ranging from 0% to 0.5%, depending on the token pair. Jupiter also charges an integrator fee whenever a third-party protocol integrates Jupiter Swap into its platform.

Jupiter spends 50% of its fee revenue to buy back its token, JUP. As demand for meme coins and tokenized stocks increases, JUP holders benefit directly if trades occur via the Jupiter DEX.

## Risks and verdict

During late 2024 and early 2025, when meme-coin mania was rampant, [Raydium had peak daily revenue](<https://defillama.com/protocol/raydium-amm?groupBy=weekly&revenue=true&tvl=false>) of $4 to $8 million, while [Pump.fun genera](<https://defillama.com/protocol/pump.fun?groupBy=weekly&revenue=true>)ted $14 to $43 million in daily revenue, according to data from DefiLlama. For comparison, Raydium averaged about $800,000 in daily revenue in September and October 2026, while Pump.fun saw about $8 million in daily revenue during the same period.

In September 2026, total tokenized stock trading volume on [Solana crossed $4.4 billion](<https://x.com/solana/status/2106768200264241173>), and the crypto bull run hadn't even started yet. When Bitcoin hits new highs in the coming years and crypto hype returns, these numbers will become mind-boggling, and the infrastructure companies powering tokenized stocks and their meme-coin pairings will likely see increased revenue.

However, new companies could emerge that tokenize stocks. We could see new launchpads, liquidity providers, and trading venues that could take a slice of existing players' profits. This could affect the revenue of the companies dominating the sector now and their native tokens.

New regulations could also affect how these companies operate. Even though most of these platforms are audited, hacks could still happen. [Pons V2 audit is underway](<https://github.com/ponsfork/pons-fork-v2>) as of October 10.

Despite these risks, investing in these companies’ tokens is likely less risky than trying to identify the few meme-coin winners among the tens of thousands launched daily, most of which never leave the bonding curve. Infrastructure tokens avoid the high failure rate of meme-coin launches while giving investors exposure to revenue-generating platforms. They still carry token-price and platform risks, though.

As with any crypto investment, do your due diligence and never invest more than you can afford to lose.
