01

The beginning

The gate at Eris

Humanity’s golden age began when deep-space explorers discovered that the dwarf planet Eris was an ancient stargate.

After a research team activated the gate, probes went out to discover a vast network of star systems with habitable planets—some richer than Earth. To finance exploration and colonization, privately owned megacorporations were formed. Despite their seemingly altruistic goals, the megacorps shifted their focus to exploiting every resource beyond Eris for private gain. Soon, everyone who worked for them became corporate citizens and settled these worlds as the first colonists.

02

Expansion

Worlds became empires

Beyond Sol, the United Nations of Earth founded the Core Systems. The earliest corporations established homeworlds there and prepared to expand farther. In the decades that followed, new star systems were colonized and became known as the Inner Systems.

Fueled by almost infinite resources, the megacorporations grew larger. From the Inner Systems, they built empires spanning multiple stars in what became known as the Outer Systems.

Citizenship followed employment. Power followed ownership.
03

Ancient inheritance

The Rhean legacy

As humanity ventured into the unknown, remnants of an ancient civilization known as the Rheans began to emerge. Their programmable metamaterial, rhillium, became part of human technology, enabling artificial gravity and advanced weapons and armor.

The explorers never encountered a living Rhean. Still, an influential part of human governance on Earth created the Human Intelligence and Research Agency—HIRA—to protect humanity. HIRA infiltrated every layer of civilization: megacorp governments, remote research stations, and the private military sector.

Agency mandate Ensure humanity continues to exist.
04

Four years of war

The Liberation

Not everyone accepted a civilization in which corporations owned the colonies—and citizenship itself.

A revolt began in the Elysium Star System and spread to neighboring systems controlled by Hyperion Industries, the largest mining megacorporation humanity had ever created. Organized civilian groups rallied under Aides Eita, the former UN envoy to Elysium.

In the opening battle, Aides Eita kidnapped Kora, the only daughter of Ceres Demetra, CEO of Aeterna Agrotech. The rebels formed the Liberation Army and fought the corporations’ autonomous security forces. After months of fierce fighting, the Army overthrew corporate governance and established the first Colonial Republic: Elisea.

Aides Eita became the first president of a republic that lasted ten days.

05

Corporate counteroffensive

The fall of Elisea

Fearing for their survival, the most influential megacorporations—led by Hyperion Industries and its CEO, Mithras Aethra—financed Trident Security Corporation and Dragon Armada to build the largest private army and navy humanity had ever seen. Hyperion began retaking the Republic’s planets in a long, bloody campaign.

The Liberation Army’s guerrilla tactics held off the overwhelming corporate forces for almost four years. Elisea’s end came during a major battle on Arcadia, when a Rhean superweapon detonated and killed or disfigured millions of civilians across the Elysium system.

The Republic claimed a Pyrrhic victory through the bravery of the Death Brigades, led by the young General Harris Ultor. But the civilian population no longer supported the war. As allegiance shifted, Hyperion Industries recaptured every system it had controlled before the conflict.

06

Beyond the blocked gate

The Underspace

Every ship that entered the gate disappeared. None returned.

President Aides Eita led a large part of the Liberation Army into the Acheron System and blocked the gate behind them. Acheron and Eleusis became known as the Underspace Systems: the place where dead souls travel and never return.

What remained of the Liberation Army either surrendered or was destroyed by Trident Security and Dragon Armada. The United Nations of Earth assumed governance of Elysium, making it the only UN-controlled system beyond the Core Systems, and helped broker the peace agreement.

Former soldiers who laid down their weapons could become citizens and work under Hyperion Industries. Yet reports of personal vendettas persisted. Ships and citizens were blacklisted, leaving them unable to work anywhere in corporate space.

07

Eleven years later

Beyond the digital veil

The megacorporations appeared to have secured lasting peace across colonized space. The appearance was beginning to fracture.

Remnants of the Liberation Army began conspiring again. Civilians were fed constant entertainment to keep them content and required to use brain-chip interfaces that promised to “improve productivity” and “seamlessly integrate with all technology.”

Some began to see beyond the digital veil. Led by an elusive figure named Mercer—hidden beneath a black banner with a white star—a new group of rebels prepared for another war. This time, they intended to free humanity across all colonized space.
